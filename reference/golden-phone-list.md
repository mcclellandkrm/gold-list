# The Golden Phone List

*A tiered SoC-first buying guide for used smartphones in 2026. UK-focused pricing.*

---

## How to use this list

Sellers rarely name the chip. So the workflow is:

1. **Find the chip tier you want** from the list below.
2. **Search by model/model-number**, not specs.
3. **Cross-check the price** against the "hone in" threshold. Under threshold = worth a closer look.
4. **Verify condition and provenance** using the Pre-Purchase Checklist further down.

Alongside the chip, you want a minimum of **8 GB RAM / 128 GB storage** (flagships should be 12 GB / 256 GB+). Unlike laptops, phone RAM and storage can't be upgraded, so this is fixed at purchase.

**Software support matters as much as the chip.** See the Software Support Hierarchy section below.

## Chips to Avoid (or be wary of)

- **Snapdragon 8 Gen 1** (non-plus) — runs hot, throttles aggressively. Samsung S22 UK, OnePlus 10 Pro, Xiaomi 12.
- **Exynos 990** — Samsung S20 UK, Note 20 UK. Battery life and thermals are poor.
- **Exynos 2100 / 2200** — the bad old Exynos. S21 and S22 UK variants. Skip.
- **MediaTek Helio G-series** — budget tier, will feel slow by 2026.
- **Snapdragon 4-series, 6-series (pre-Gen 3)** — low-end; avoid unless you genuinely only need calls and messages.
- **Unisoc anything** — skip.

**Rule of thumb for Samsung UK buyers:** S20, S21, S22 are the Exynos-damage years. S23 onwards have either global Snapdragon (S23) or competitive Exynos (S24). Pre-2020 Exynos was fine too (S10, Note 10). It's the three-year stretch in the middle you want to avoid.

## Feature Differentiators

*Non-chip things that determine which phone in a tier is the right phone.*

### LiDAR Scanner (iPhones only)

Apple added a **LiDAR scanner** to Pro iPhones starting with the iPhone 12 Pro. It's a small laser depth sensor next to the camera lens.

**Which iPhones have it:**

- iPhone 12 Pro / 12 Pro Max
- iPhone 13 Pro / 13 Pro Max
- iPhone 14 Pro / 14 Pro Max
- iPhone 15 Pro / 15 Pro Max
- iPhone 16 Pro / 16 Pro Max
- iPad Pro (2020 and later)

**Which don't:** Every base, Plus, mini, SE, and 16e iPhone. Every Android phone (the feature is Apple-exclusive on phones; some Android devices use ToF sensors which are similar but not identical).

**What it actually does:**

- **Faster, more accurate autofocus in low light** — the laser measures distance in milliseconds rather than relying on contrast detection
- **Night mode portraits** (only works on LiDAR-equipped iPhones)
- **Room scanning apps** — Polycam, Canvas, Apple's RoomPlan API. Genuinely useful for renovation, interior design, real estate, 3D modelling
- **AR apps work better** — object placement is more accurate, occlusion is handled properly (virtual objects can go behind real ones)
- **Accessibility** — People Detection in Magnifier app uses LiDAR to tell blind/low-vision users how far away people are

**Who should care:** photographers, anyone doing AR/3D work, anyone renovating or measuring spaces, accessibility users. If that's you, it's a real reason to pick a Pro model over the base.

**Who shouldn't pay extra for it:** casual users who mostly shoot in daylight and don't do AR. Easy to forget it's even there.

### Carrier Locking

Some used iPhones (especially US-sourced) are still locked to a specific carrier. Dial `*#06#` and cross-check with an IMEI unlock checker, or ask the seller to confirm it's unlocked. A locked phone is ~30% cheaper but only useful on that carrier.

### eSIM-Only iPhones

iPhone 14 and later sold in the **US** are eSIM-only (no physical SIM tray). They work fine on UK networks, but if you prefer physical SIMs you'll need a UK, EU, or most other regions' variant. Check the model number on Apple's support page to be sure.

### Wireless Charging / MagSafe

All modern flagships have Qi wireless charging. **MagSafe** (magnetic attachment + faster wireless charging) is iPhone 12 and later only. iPhone 16 added Qi2 compatibility, which means magnetic Qi2 accessories now work universally.

### IP Rating

Most flagships are IP68 (submersion-proof). Most mid-range phones are IP54 (splash-proof only). Worth checking if you're clumsy around water.

## Software Support Hierarchy (in 2026)

How long the phone will keep getting security updates, roughly, from launch:

| Tier | Years of Support | Examples |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Best | 7 years | Samsung Galaxy S24, S23, Pixel 8 and later, Pixel 9a |
| Excellent | 5–6 years | iPhones (varies by launch year; iOS typically supports ~5-year-old devices) |
| Good | 4 years | OnePlus 11/12, Nothing Phone (2) |
| Adequate | 3 years | Most Xiaomi, older OnePlus, older Nothing, most Motorola |
| Ending soon | <1 year remaining | iPhone 11, SE 2, Pixel 6 series, Samsung S20 series |

**Key point:** Android major version updates usually run out *before* security patches do. A phone may stop getting new Android versions at year 3 but still receive security patches to year 5. Security patches are what matter for safety.

**Apple's pattern:** iOS supports roughly the last 5–6 generations. In 2026 that means iPhone 11 and SE 2 are on their last legs; iPhone 12 is safe through iOS 19 at least; iPhone 13 and later are completely safe for years.

## When to Overrule Chip Tier

A phone's chip sets its performance ceiling. Once the chip is "good enough" for your actual workload — messaging, browsing, camera, social apps, light gaming — the rest of the package often matters more than another 10–15% of CPU performance.

The factors that can outrank chip tier:

- **Display refresh rate** — 120Hz vs 60Hz is a bigger daily-use difference than most chip gaps. Scrolling, animations, gaming all feel markedly more modern.
- **Software support remaining** — a phone whose security patches ended last year is a worse buy than one with a mediocre chip and three years of patches left.
- **Modern connectivity** — 5G, Wi-Fi 6/7, wireless charging, NFC for contactless payments.
- **Camera pipeline** — the 2022 mid-range image-processing stack often beats the 2019 flagship stack, even with similar sensors on paper. Computational photography has matured rapidly.
- **IP rating** — older flagships often have none; modern mid-range phones often have IP53 or IP67+.
- **Battery capacity and health** — a newer phone with a fresh battery beats an older phone sitting at 78% health.
- **Build and ergonomics** — size, weight, grip, button placement, material quality. Subjective but real.

**Rules of thumb:**

- Comparing a **2019 flagship to a 2022 mid-range**? The mid-range usually wins on real-world experience.
- Comparing a **2022 flagship to a 2024 flagship** of the same tier? The 2022 is often the better-value buy.
- Comparing **two phones within the same tier**? Chip is a wash — decide on ecosystem, screen size, remaining software support, camera priorities, and design feel.
- A phone whose **security updates have ended** is effectively a dead phone for anything financial or personal, regardless of how fast its chip is.

**Worked example.** Samsung Galaxy S10 Lite (Snapdragon 855, 60Hz, 4G only, EOL for updates) vs Nothing Phone (1) (Snapdragon 778G+, 120Hz, 5G, wireless charging, still receiving patches). On chip alone the S10 Lite wins. On everything else — display, connectivity, camera, software support, efficiency — the Nothing wins. Net result: the Nothing is the better phone, despite the apparent chip-tier downgrade.

**The Golden List tells you which chips are worth hunting. This section tells you when to ignore it.**

## The Model-Number Search Trick

Sellers often list the model number but not the SoC. Use it to confirm region/chip:

- **Samsung S24** — SM-S921**B** (UK, Exynos) / SM-S921**U** (US, Snapdragon)
- **Samsung S23** — SM-S911**B** (UK) / SM-S911**U** (US). S23 uses 8 Gen 2 globally — no Exynos variant.
- **Samsung S22** — SM-S901**B** (UK, Exynos 2200 — avoid) / SM-S901**U** (US, SD 8 Gen 1 — also avoid). S22 is the generation to skip.
- **Samsung S21** — SM-G991**B** (UK, Exynos 2100 — avoid) / SM-G991**U** (US, SD 888).
- **Samsung S20** — SM-G980/G981**B** (UK, Exynos 990 — avoid) / SM-G981**U** (US, SD 865).
- **iPhone** — model number on the back (e.g., A2882 = iPhone 14 Pro). Cross-reference on Apple's support site.
- **Pixel** — G1MNW = 8, G0B96 = 8 Pro, GKWS6 = 9, GX7AS = 7 Pro, etc. Google's support pages list these.
- **OnePlus 11** — CPH2449 (global) / PHB110 (China).

## Pre-Purchase Checklist

Before handing over money, check:

### Condition & Battery

- **Battery health.** iPhone: Settings → Battery → Battery Health (aim for >85%). Android: dial `*#*#4636#*#*` or use AccuBattery for a few days.
- **Screen.** Full-white and full-black image test for dead pixels and burn-in (AMOLED panels, especially on older Samsungs and Pixels).
- **Water damage indicator.** iPhone: in the SIM tray. Should be white, not red.
- **Charging port + speakers.** Dust, bent pins, muffled audio.
- **All buttons + Face ID / fingerprint.** Test every one.

### Provenance & Locks

- **IMEI status.** Dial `*#06#` to get the IMEI. Check it on the UK blocked-IMEI checker (CheckMEND) — confirms not reported stolen.
- **Activation lock (iPhone).** Must be signed out of iCloud (Find My off). If the seller can't do this in front of you, walk away.
- **Google account lock (Android).** Must be signed out of Google account and factory reset completed.
- **Carrier lock.** Test a SIM from your carrier in-store before paying.

### Apple-specific

- **Service History / Parts-Paired warnings.** Settings → General → About → scroll for "Parts and Service History". iPhones with third-party screen, battery, or camera replacements show warnings and may lose features: **non-genuine screen = no True Tone, no auto-brightness calibration**; **non-genuine battery = no battery health reporting**; **non-genuine camera = some features disabled**. Not automatically a deal-breaker, but should reduce the price.
- **eSIM-only check.** US iPhone 14+ variants have no physical SIM tray.

### Samsung-specific

- **Knox fuse.** If a Samsung phone has ever been rooted, its Knox fuse is permanently blown. This disables Samsung Pay, Secure Folder, Samsung Health fitness sync, and blocks many banking apps. Check Settings → Biometrics and Security, or dial `*#*#4636#*#*`. A blown Knox fuse should knock 20%+ off the price.

### Grey Imports (especially Xiaomi, Vivo, OPPO, Honor, iQOO)

- **Network band support.** UK needs Band 20 (800 MHz) for reliable 4G rural coverage, and Band 28 (700 MHz) for some 5G. Chinese domestic variants often omit these. Check the exact model's band list at gsmarena.com or willmyphonework.net before buying.
- **Google services.** Chinese domestic variants often ship without Google Play Services pre-installed. You can usually sideload but it's a hassle.
- **No UK warranty.** Grey imports have no UK retailer-warranty rights.

### Refurbished Buying (Back Market, Music Magpie, reBuy, Swappa)

Condition grades are broadly standardised but not identical across sellers:

- **Grade A / Excellent / "Premium"** — looks like new, battery ≥90%. Pay roughly 85–90% of the "hone in" threshold.
- **Grade B / Good** — light scratches on screen or body, battery ≥80%. Pay 70–80% of threshold.
- **Grade C / Fair** — visible wear, sometimes dings or dents, battery may be 75%+. Pay 50–65% of threshold. Often the best value if cosmetics don't matter.
- **Warranty.** Back Market and Music Magpie both offer 12 months refurb warranty. eBay/Gumtree private sales = zero. Weigh the price difference accordingly.
